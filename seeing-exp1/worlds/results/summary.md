1. 40 worlds, 0 failed runs, every world loaded. Working in the end: GPT-6.1 Sol 17 of 20, Opus 5.5 13 of 20. Working as first written, before any readback: 16 and 12.
2. Most worlds work on the first write. The shot and the cup worked 16 of 16 as first written, for both models and both arms; GPT-6.1 Sol's door 4 of 4. The model writing a world from one sentence is mostly not the bottleneck.
3. The picture fixed 2 of the 6 worlds that failed as first written; text only fixed 0 of 6. The two arms are identical until the first readback, so this is the comparison that measures the readback.
4. Both fixes came from seeing what did not happen. Opus 5.5's door (picture, seed 1): "No fan of residue copies swings in from the open position", so it worked out that MJCF reads the hinge range in degrees and added `angle="radian"`. GPT-6.1 Sol's stack (picture, seed 1): "the five-block stack remains upright rather than toppling", so it pushed harder.
5. Without a readback the models do not look again. Text only tried a fix in 1 of 6 failing worlds and claimed every one of its 6 final failures works. With the picture, 3 of 4 final failures were still claimed to work: Opus's other radian door, where it saw a shut door and called it right, and two catapults.
6. The degrees trap caught Opus 3 times in 4 doors (range "0 1.6" read as 1.6°, so the limit snaps the open door shut). The test written before the runs caught all 3. GPT-6.1 Sol wrote radians correctly in 4 of 4.
7. The catapult was the hardest brief: 2 of 8 work under the pre-registered test. Three failures miss only on "a bucket 3 m away", measured from where the ball starts: their buckets are 3.60 to 3.75 m from the ball and 3.15 to 3.19 m from the catapult. Measured from the catapult (`results/sensitivity.md`), 4 of 8 work, and GPT-6.1 Sol's one pre-registered pass fails at 2.49 m. The brief is ambiguous; the pre-registered verdicts stand.
8. First embarrassment test, passed: with the picture, Opus 5.5 got 4 of 5 briefs working within three rounds (all but the catapult) and GPT-6.1 Sol 5 of 5.
9. Second embarrassment test, mostly true: on the stack and the catapult, text only did nearly as well as the picture. Pre-registered, working in the end: Opus picture 2 of 4 against text 1 of 4, GPT-6.1 Sol 3 of 4 against 2 of 4. But of those one-world leads, only GPT-6.1 Sol's stack fix came from a picture; Opus's stack lead (2 of 2 against 0 of 2) and GPT-6.1 Sol's catapult lead were decided at the first write, before either arm saw anything.
10. Cost: $8.30 for 40 worlds (Opus 5.5 $6.73, GPT-6.1 Sol $1.58), against the $33 estimate, because most worlds stopped after one round. About 2.5 minutes a world. With experiments 1 and 1b, $48.75 of the $100 cap.
11. So the picture's value is narrow but real: it lets a model see that something did not happen, which text cannot. To measure it properly, the next test needs worlds that fail more often as first written, more seeds, and briefs whose words fix a single measurement.

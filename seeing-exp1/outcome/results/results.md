## Summary

1. 144 runs, 0 failed cells. GPT-6.1 Sol named the miss and made the shot after one correction in 72 of 72, in every readback, text only included. Opus 5.5 named 63 of 72 and made 60 of 72 after one correction.
2. Text only was enough. With no readback at all, GPT-6.1 Sol named 12 of 12 and made 12 of 12; Opus 5.5 named 11 of 12 and made 12 of 12 after one correction, as with numbers and the camera at 64 px. Both worked the flight out from the keyframe with drag. For a ballistic shot, thinking it through is enough, which is the first of the design's "what each result would mean".
3. Numbers were Opus's best readback: 12 of 12 named, made after one and after two. GPT-6.1 Sol was 12 of 12 there too. State beats pixels for Opus; for GPT-6.1 Sol every readback is at the ceiling, so this task cannot rank them.
4. For Opus the pictures hurt, and 128 px hurt most. Camera 128 px: named 9 of 12, made after one 5 of 12, after two 7 of 12. Drafting 128 px: 8, 9 and 7 of 12. At 64 px it did better (camera 12, 12, 12; drafting 11, 10, 10), because it leaned on the text: it cited "text" alone in 5 of 12 camera runs and 2 of 12 drafting runs at 64 px, and in none at 128.
5. All 9 of Opus's wrong calls were "long": 5 on its own made shot (none) and 4 on the left miss, one of those in text only. In 7 of the 9 it gave the same no-drag estimate, the ball "about 1 m above the rim" at the hoop's distance, and described the picture as agreeing. That is the shot it designed by integrating the drag, which MuJoCo puts 1.4 cm from the rim's center.
6. All 12 of Opus's failed first corrections were in picture readbacks, and all fell short, by 0.26 to 0.52 m. Shown its own short correction on turn 3, it caught 3 of 12 and said "goes in" for 9. It also called a made correction a miss 4 times. GPT-6.1 Sol judged its turn-3 readback right 60 of 60.
7. First embarrassment test, passed but not by the picture: at 64 px from experiment 1's camera, both models told short from long 6 of 6. But text only did as well, and GPT-6.1 Sol cited the picture alone once in 12 camera-64 runs. The pass shows the picture did not get in the way, not that it did the work.
8. Second embarrassment test, failed: the drafting view did no better than the camera at the same pixel count. GPT-6.1 Sol: 12 of 12 for both, at both sizes. Opus 5.5 named 8 vs 9 of 12 at 128 px and 11 vs 12 at 64 px, and made after two 7 vs 7 and 10 vs 12, drafting against camera. The drafting plan view is the only picture that shows a left miss, but the left miss is also a nonzero sideways velocity in the text, so nothing needed the view.
9. The text gives away more than intended: the left miss is a y velocity in the keyframe, and every edited qvel carries four decimals where the unedited "none" scene keeps Opus's "3.21 0 9.3". That tells a model whether the shot was changed, not which way, so short and long still had to be worked out. Neither is a harness bug, and neither changes a verdict, but both make text only easier than the design meant.
10. Cost: the 128 runs in this batch cost $28.29; experiment 1b came to $32.82 in all (Opus 5.5 $23.80 including $0.18 to author the shot, GPT-6.1 Sol $9.03), about $0.33 a run for Opus and $0.13 for GPT-6.1 Sol. With experiment 1, $40.45 of the $100 cap.
11. So for a clean ballistic shot the picture is not needed, and Opus does better without it. To test what a readback is for, the outcome must depend on something the text cannot settle by calculation: contacts (rim, board), many bodies, or a scene the model did not write.
